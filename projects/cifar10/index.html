Teaching a network to tell a cat from a dog.

A convolutional neural network built from scratch in PyTorch, with no pre-trained weights, that sorts small 32×32 photos into ten classes: planes, cars, birds, cats, deer, dogs, frogs, horses, ships and trucks.

The problem

CIFAR-10 has 60,000 tiny colour images. Guessing at random gets 10% right. The rules of the coursework were to design and train my own network, without borrowing a model someone else had already trained, and to run on a CPU.

The model

Three convolution blocks pick out increasingly complex shapes, then two fully connected layers make the decision.

Conv 3→32   → BatchNorm → ReLU → MaxPool    32×32 → 16×16
Conv 32→64  → BatchNorm → ReLU → MaxPool    16×16 → 8×8
Conv 64→128 → BatchNorm → ReLU → MaxPool    8×8   → 4×4
Flatten (2,048) → Linear 256 → ReLU → Dropout 0.5 → Linear 10

  • Batch normalisation

    Keeps each layer's outputs on a steady scale, so training is faster and more stable.

  • Dropout

    Randomly switches off half the neurons in the dense layer during training so the network can't just memorise images.

  • Data augmentation

    Training images are randomly flipped and shifted, so the model sees a slightly different picture every epoch.

  • Learning-rate schedule

    Adam starting at 0.001, halved every 10 epochs, so the model takes big steps early and fine steps late.

Training and validation

I split the 50,000 training images 40,000 / 10,000 into training and validation sets, with a fixed random seed so the split is reproducible. The official 10,000-image test set was kept aside and only used at the end. Validation loss and accuracy were logged every epoch and a checkpoint was saved every 5 epochs.

Vehicles and frogs are easy: they have distinctive shapes and colours. Cats are by far the hardest. At 32×32 pixels a cat, a dog and a small deer look very alike, so that's where most mistakes happen.

What I'd do next

Go deeper where it's weakest.

A fourth convolution block or residual connections would help separate cats from dogs.

Train for longer on a GPU.

Training was capped at 20 epochs on a CPU. More epochs and a GPU would show how much headroom the model still has.

Look at the confusion matrix, not just the score.

The per-class breakdown above says more about the model than one overall number.
